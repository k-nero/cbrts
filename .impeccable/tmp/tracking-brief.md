# Surface brief: Customer request tracking

Mode: Operate. Customer (club manager or horse owner) on desktop, 1440 px. Delivered as Figma screens in the EquiFreight file, generated by docs/figma-plugin/src/06-tracking.js.

## Job
Answer "Do I need to do anything?" first and "Is the move on track?" second, from submission to handover. Arrivals come from notifications (quotation issued, document required, cleared border) or from My requests. Checked occasionally over 2–6 weeks, closely on travel day.

## Scope
My requests list, plus the detail page in four states: in review, documents needed, in transit, delivered. New components: Owed item, Stage row, Route stop. Existing screens and components stay as they are. The header's "Quotes" item is relabelled "My requests" on these screens only, as an instance override.

## Decisions
- Staff appear as first name + role ("Minh, driver").
- The delivered state owes "Confirm handover and condition", which closes the job.
- Anti-goals: map tiles or a moving dot; estimated arrival times finer than the checkpoints; a single price before a quote; a chat-style timeline; burying "You owe" below the status; modals for uploads.

## Direction contract
THESIS: The page is the customer's to-do list for the move, with the move's status underneath it. It refuses the category default, a status header over a timeline that leaves the customer hunting for what they owe.
OWN-WORLD: The EquiFreight world as it stands: navy canvas, gold only on the one forward action, and status always as tone plus word. Fraunces for places and names, Outfit for prose, DM Mono for codes, dates and deadlines. Every deadline is shown twice, as time remaining and as a date.
STORY: The visitor learns what they owe, why each item is needed (the rule, or who asked and why), and by when. They act in place. Then they see which stage the move is in, who owns the next step and when, and where the horses physically are at the last confirmed checkpoint.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Header, then the route as the title with the job code and departure beneath it. The stage and gate chips sit top right. Directly under that is the full-width "You owe N items" panel: items sorted by deadline, overdue first, each with a reason, a two-line deadline and its own action button, all visible without scrolling. The primary action is the top item's button.
